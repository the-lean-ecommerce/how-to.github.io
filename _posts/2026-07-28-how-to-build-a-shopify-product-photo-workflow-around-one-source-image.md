## 1. Start With The Cleanest Source Image

Choose the photo that already shows the product clearly from the angle you would trust on a product page. Avoid heavy shadows, motion blur, and cluttered props. If a product has multiple candidate images, pick the one that keeps edges readable and materials visible.

The reason this matters is simple: AI editing works best when the base image is honest. You want the app to improve the image, not guess what the product looks like. ## 2. Clean The Base Shot Before You Branch It

Use the background remover first if the original photo has distractions around the product. Then apply upscaling or auto enhancement only when the base image needs it. The goal is to make one polished master image that can serve as the starting point for every other output.

A common mistake is to jump straight into a lifestyle scene before the base image is stable. That usually creates avoidable artifacts, especially around edges, reflections, and transparent or glossy materials. ## 3. Create Try-On Assets Only When The Category Benefits

Try-on is not for every catalog, but it is a strong fit for apparel, jewelry, accessories, and other products where the customer needs scale or fit context. If the product works on a person, create one version that answers the question, “What does this look like when someone wears it?”

Keep the try-on goal narrow. The app should show the product realistically, not redesign it. A good try-on image preserves shape, trim, and color while giving the buyer enough context to judge the item quickly.

## 4. Place The Product Into A Real Scene

Once the master image is clean, use object placement to create lifestyle versions for the homepage, product page, collection page, or campaign banner. The point is not to make every image look dramatic. The point is to answer the shopper’s context question.

A kitchen scene works when the product belongs in a kitchen. A studio counter works when the product needs a neutral premium setting. An outdoor scene works when the product gains credibility from environment. Keep the scene close to the product’s actual use so the final image still feels believable.

## 5. Turn The Same Asset Into Video And B-Roll

Use the same source image again for short videos, UGC-style clips, or b-roll. This is useful when you need ad creative fast, but do not want to start from scratch for every format. A still product shot can become a motion asset for social media, retargeting, or a launch page.

Keep the motion simple. Short clips work better when they emphasize product detail, reveal, impact, or a small contextual action. The app’s video tools are most useful when the product remains the star and the motion just gives it a little more presence.

## 6. Keep The Workflow Reviewable And Reusable

The easiest way to lose time is to treat each output like a one-off. Instead, save the clean source image, the enhanced master, and the best variants as a small repeatable set. That makes it easier to revisit the product later for seasonal updates, campaign refreshes, or new ad angles.

## Troubleshooting Common Problems

If the product changes shape, start with a cleaner source image and reduce the amount of transformation you ask for in one pass.

If the scene looks too stylized, simplify the background and keep the product closer to a real retail environment.

If a try-on image looks off, check whether the original garment or accessory is clear enough to survive placement on a model.

If the outputs feel inconsistent across channels, use the same master image and the same enhancement settings as your baseline. The more the workflow changes between channels, the more likely the visual identity will drift.
